DESIGN STAFF WORKING BRIEF · November 11, 2026 · Soundtrack direction for development together

The purpose / A soundtrack of events

Follow the journey.
Even with your eyes closed.

The goal is to create a soundtrack that carries the audience through the show’s journey—even with their eyes closed. Each important event should help that journey unfold through sound.

These ideas are guideposts—a map for developing the show’s musical identity. The important events, contrasts, and connections shape the journey. The musical suggestions offer places to begin; Mark will develop the arrangement, with room to explore how each event takes shape.

ACT I

Approx. 3:55

Fractured

Chamber-brass virtuosity. A lower-voiced solo. A full-corps jam.

The chamber-brass opening, full-corps arrivals, solo development, and closing jam are the important events in this part of the soundtrack. The suggestions below give Mark a map for developing their pacing, cadence, and varied phrase endings. The separate “Hurt” bridge from the Intro is future work outside this act’s timing.

1. Ornate, virtuosic brass

Approx. 1:25

A featured brass moment after the Intro's focus on color guard and percussion. Translate the ornate violin writing and chamber character of Berghain into brass.

Current direction / Sequence to develop
01 / CHAMBER BRASS

Chamber-brass opening

Ornate, virtuosic double-tonguing brass, with front ensemble playing the background. The group could be built around the players who can deliver the material.

02 / FULL CHOIR

Full drum-corps arrival

Use the original's full-choir moment as the reference. Contrast the small group with the loud arrival: full drum corps doing drum-corps kinds of things.

03 / MELLOPHONES

Develop the melody

The returning solo melody in the source becomes a mellophone sectional. RAH suggests playing with that melody to make it very, very mellophone-like.

04 / CHOIR RETURNS

Set up the next hit

Return to the original's full-choir sound, voiced through brass. Restore the contrast and the feeling that we're going to hit you with it again.

05 / UNISON FEATURE

Dissolve into virtuosity

Instead of the expected hit, dissolve into a 'fall out' brass moment in unison. Different feels; approximately 20 seconds. This closes the first part.

Brass Direction

The size and instrumentation are open: maybe six trumpets, two mellophones, however many baritones can handle the writing, and a kick-ass tuba player. The chamber-brass group could be built around the players who can deliver the ornate, virtuosic material.

Ensemble Coordination

Front ensemble provides the background for the chamber-brass group. The first full-choir arrival is a full drum-corps event. Percussion supports the brass-led writing; the brass carries the featured material.

Exit / Timing

The extended unison feature is the last event in this first part, after the mellophone sectional and choir return. Its approximately 20 seconds is not the duration of the entire opening. Next: the lower-voiced solo in part two.

For Mark to develop together with the design team

Tempo and notation

Could the chamber material work at quarter note = 90, with the corps pulse written at quarter note = 180? The violin's sixteenths would become eighths at the doubled written tempo, retaining the same note rate.

Extended brass feature reference

RAH suggests 2026 Vanguard as a stylistic reference. Keep the chamber character and intricate movement. The closing feature should be virtuosic, very unison, and move through different feels. The 3:43 example is linked below and in the closing overview.

2. Lower-voiced solo / the Björk moment

Approx. 1:00

Often called the ‘Björk moment’ in development, this section still features Rosalía as the main voice in the source. Björk’s voice enters at the end.

Current direction / Sequence to develop
01 / FEATURED SOLO

Lower-voiced solo

Trombone or baritone solo. Could the first phrase be a solo, then all the baritones join in the second time? Develop it into a baritone sectional moment.

02 / ACCOMPANIMENT

Ornate small group

Behind the soloist, return to the original's very ornate style. Adapt the writing for our brass players. Maybe front ensemble plays the more rooted downbeat material from the original.

03 / ENSEMBLE GROWS

Choir behind the solo

Could the full corps enter behind the amplified soloist with a halftime feel, supported by a choir patch? Combine the brass and choir sound. Maybe mess with the rhythm, but keep it a unison idea.

04 / POSSIBLE CADENZA

Cut off the ensemble

Cut off the ensemble for a drum corps-style cadenza. Maybe draw from Björk's voice or melody. It needs to be clappable and show off the musician's ability, possibly coordinated with front ensemble.

Brass Direction

Feature the lower voice clearly. Preserve the ornate character in the small-group accompaniment while tailoring the writing to our players. Build the contrast from solo and small group to the choir sound behind.

Ensemble Coordination

The current direction calls for more battery contribution. Front ensemble could play the original's rooted downbeat material. Could a choir patch support the full corps behind the soloist in the halftime feel? The cadenza could coordinate front ensemble and soloist.

Exit / Connection

The possible cadenza closes this section; it is a drum-corps soloist moment rather than a traditional cadenza. From there, move into the original groove that begins 'Do You Love Me?' The exact connection is still to be developed.

For Mark to develop together with the design team

Soloist and accompaniment

Develop playable ornate accompaniment, the unison rhythm, and how the choir sound enters behind the trombone or baritone soloist.

Cadenza and transition

Maybe draw the cadenza from Björk's voice or melody and coordinate it with front ensemble. Make it clappable and let it show off the musician's ability. The duration and handoff into the groove remain open.

3. Do You Love Me?

Approx. 1:30

Fun, something the corps can identify with, and a full-corps moment. Build a really cool jam. This closes the soundtrack for Act I: Fractured.

Current direction / Sequence to develop
01 / START THE GROOVE

Percussion / rhythm section

Percussion / rhythm section establishes the groove. From there, layer in the next ensemble moments.

02 / ADD THE BRASS

Low brass, then high

Layer in low brass, followed by the rest of the high brass. Build the ensemble into the groove.

03 / ORNATE ACTIVITY

Mellophones + front ensemble

These voices could take on the woodwind-like activity described by the reference. Build increasingly dense activity against the groove.

04 / LAYER THE WORDS

'Do you love me?'

Add the words over the top with the rap feel. Keep building the jam into the full-corps moment.

Original Music For Mark

For Mark to develop: an original soundtrack groove for this layered section. Urban Light is a placeholder and musical descriptor for groove, layering and buildup. The intention is to develop original material rather than arrange or quote that piece.

Reference / Around 3:49

James M. David - Urban Light. The reference is the woodwind activity against the low-brass and percussion groove. Use it to understand the proposed layering and feel.

Lyric / Emotional Intent

Replace the original explicit rap line with 'Do you love me?' It must be a question, carrying love, care and connection after conflict. The musical experience is fun and shared; the question remains at the end of Fractured.

For Mark to develop together with the design team

Develop the original groove

Build the original musical foundation and test the proposed entrances: rhythm section, low brass, high brass, mellophones/front-ensemble activity, then the words. The detailed orchestration remains open.

Shape the payoff

Percussion becomes a primary driver. Work out how the layers accumulate, how the words sit over the groove, and how the jam reaches its full-corps payoff within the act's overall 3:55 duration.
